How this table is counted and interpreted
Population
Freeze method IDs in table1-population.json. One method record is the counting unit. All applicable methodological_components contribute once to their columns. VAE is nested within probabilistic models. Columns overlap. Unresolved membership in a particular block is excluded from that column and reported separately. A method still contributes to every confirmed block.
Workflow
Classify the documented method workflow, including supported training and inference stages. Preserve stage-specific notes. Properties in one profile need not be simultaneously available in every configuration. A downstream evaluation alone does not add a building block or training target to the integration model.
Counting
For each row and column, n is the number of positive documented profiles and N is the number classified positive or scoped negative for that property. Exclude not-assessed from N and list excluded methods. A known not-applicable property is a scoped negative and remains in N. If N=0, display NA and do not color the cell. Column-membership uncertainty is distinct from an unassessed row property: both exclusions are explicitly recorded.
Negative
A scoped negative means the property is not documented in the reviewed workflow or is inapplicable to that workflow. It does not assert impossibility, or prove absence from every implementation. An incomplete field remains not-assessed, never negative.
Profile mapping
For the established categorical axes, a known field is a curated complete assignment of applicable values for the profiled workflow. Membership in the stated values is positive. Other known values are scoped negatives. Any unassessed field is excluded. The two uncertainty rows use the source-audited per-method statuses in table1-evidence.json.
Frequency
Compute p=100*n/N. Determine bins from the unrounded fraction: 0<=p<25, 25<=p<50, 50<=p<75, 75<=p<=100. Display n/N and rounded whole percent. Small denominators remain visible. Do not interpret these bands as performance or suitability scores. Frequency categories are descriptive conventions, not statistical confidence levels.
Interpretation
This is a descriptive summary of the illustrative manuscript corpus, not an estimate of all published methods. No confidence interval or population prevalence claim is implied. Multi-block profiles describe combinations, so a property cannot be attributed causally to one block.
Provenance
Preserve source URLs, locators, rationales, date, baseline source commit, frozen IDs and input hashes. Store cell membership and excluded-method lists. Regenerate outputs from the same machine-readable definitions after any curation change.
Review
Evidence extraction and the additional audit were AI-assisted. Sources and decisions are retained for author review. Automated consistency checks test arithmetic and data integrity, not scientific truth.
Building-block definitions for every column
Deterministic optimization
A dedicated point-estimate integration construction, such as matrix factorization, matching/transport, network fusion, direct latent optimization or cross-modal target regression. Generic numerical optimization, a point prediction head or ordinary neural reconstruction within another block does not supply this tag by itself. Random initialization and stochastic minibatches do not make the model probabilistic.
Probabilistic graphical models
An explicit probabilistic model of observations and latent quantities used in the integration workflow. Point estimates of some parameters are compatible with this block. A probability-valued matching weight or discriminative score alone is insufficient.
Variational autoencoders
An encoder approximates a latent posterior and is trained with a variational reconstruction/regularization objective. Every VAE also belongs to the probabilistic column. Deterministic autoencoders and VAE-named baseline files do not qualify.
Contrastive learning
A contrastive objective compares related and nonmatching observations or representations, or an explicitly documented negative-free contrastive construction. A generic similarity penalty alone is insufficient.
Foundation models
The method develops a pretrained representation on a broad, diverse corpus for reuse or adaptation in distinct downstream analyses. Pretraining may initialize from existing encoders. Using an external pretrained backbone, task-specific contrastive pretraining, or evaluation of the same prediction task on another cohort alone does not qualify. The evidence must describe the pretraining corpus and intended representation reuse. Corpus breadth is assessed in its biological domain, without a universal sample-count cutoff.
Set representation and multiple instance learning
An integral aggregation step summarizes variable-size collections of observed subunits, such as cells or image patches, into a fixed-dimensional representation for integration. Permutation-invariant or explicitly location-aware set aggregation can qualify. MIL is the subset trained with bag-level labels and does not require instance labels. Mean pooling and attention can both qualify. Ordinary fixed-grid image tokenization or incidental preprocessing alone does not qualify.
Operational definitions for every row
Correspondence: Paired data
The documented workflow uses known links between measurements of the same observational units. This denotes supported pairing, not a requirement that every unit be fully profiled. Methods may also support partial or absent pairing.
Correspondence: Weak linkage
Shared or biologically related features, spatial context or other linking information connect modalities without establishing measured pairs of observational units.
Correspondence: Unpaired observations
The workflow integrates observations without known cross-modal unit pairs. Learned matches do not retroactively make the input paired.
Missingness: Entry-level missingness
The method explicitly handles unavailable individual measurements within an otherwise observed input modality. Differences between fixed assay feature panels and ordinary observed zeros do not alone establish missing-input support. The supported input pattern and fitting or inference stage are recorded.
Missingness: Modality-level missingness
The documented fitting or inference procedure obtains the integrated representation or model output from available input modalities when another input modality is absent for a unit. The supported input pattern and stage are recorded. Predicting a prespecified target assay from complete source inputs does not by itself establish missing-input support. Absent measured unit pairs alone do not qualify.
Granularity: Single-level observations
Integrated inputs are represented at one observational level, such as matched cells or patient-level profiles.
Granularity: Multi-level observations
The integration explicitly relates nested or different observational levels, such as patches within patients or cells contributing to tissue locations. Routine within-modality preprocessing alone does not qualify.
Model transparency: Explicit mappings or loadings
Documented loadings, mappings or couplings directly connect the represented quantities. This does not by itself certify biological correctness or complete interpretability.
Model transparency: Explicit likelihoods
A stated likelihood specifies a measurement distribution in the integrated model. A numerical loss that can be given a probabilistic interpretation is not sufficient without such a model specification.
Model transparency: Inspectable neural structure
Neural components have documented roles that can be inspected, such as separated latent branches, biologically structured decoders or attention aggregation. Attention alone is not a causal explanation.
Model transparency: Post-hoc inspection
The reviewed workflow documents an analysis of fitted representations or predictions, such as latent traversal or attribution. Availability of generic external explanation tools does not qualify automatically.
Modality decomposition: Shared and modality-specific
The model explicitly distinguishes variation common to modalities from modality-specific variation. A biological/technical split or one joint embedding alone does not qualify.
Modality decomposition: Shared by subsets of modalities
The model resolves components active in a proper subset of at least two among three or more modalities. A two-modality shared/private split is not sufficient.
Prior biological knowledge: Explicit biological knowledge
External biological structure or measured spatial/temporal context is explicitly used in model construction or fitting. The property is not inferred from post-hoc biological interpretation or a graph learned solely from the integrated features.
Method scope: General-purpose
The methodological construction is not restricted to one data domain or endpoint, although reported applications can be narrower. This is the curated design scope, not proof of universal validity.
Method scope: Domain-specific
The construction uses assumptions or representations designed for a defined data domain, while supporting several analyses within that domain.
Method scope: Task-specific
The construction is organized around a defined inference or prediction task rather than a broadly reusable representation.
Supervision: Unsupervised
The profiled stage learns structure without externally supplied outcome or class labels. Reconstruction- or likelihood-only fitting is placed here for consistency with the review.
Supervision: Self-supervised
Training targets are constructed from the observations or their known correspondence, such as contrastive pairs or masked measurements. This review does not separately tag ordinary likelihood/reconstruction fitting as self-supervision.
Supervision: Semi-supervised
External labels supervise only part of the training observations or an explicitly designated subset of latent factors, while other observations or factors remain unlabelled or unguided. The evidence distinguishes these two uses. Partial modality pairing alone is not semi-supervision on this axis.
Supervision: Supervised
Observed target variables supervise prediction for the modelled units, including phenotype or annotation labels and an explicitly predicted target assay. Reconstruction of the observed inputs and constructed contrastive or masking targets follow the unsupervised/self-supervised conventions above. Bag-level labels supervising unlabelled constituent instances are classified as weakly supervised instead. Both tags require separately documented stages or objectives. A classifier used only to evaluate frozen embeddings does not establish supervision of representation learning.
Supervision: Weakly supervised
Labels are coarser or less direct than the instance-level quantities being learned, for example patient outcomes supervising patch aggregation.
Integration effect: Representation and discovery
The workflow returns representations, networks, programs or composition estimates for exploratory biological analysis.
Integration effect: Alignment and correspondence
The workflow estimates cross-modal correspondences or an alignment, beyond simply combining already matched measurements.
Integration effect: Imputation
The workflow estimates unavailable entries in a measurement block, including documented within-modality recovery. Reconstruction of fully observed data alone does not establish imputation.
Integration effect: Cross-modal prediction
One or more observed modalities predict a different, unavailable target modality.
Integration effect: Generation
The workflow explicitly samples or generates new measurements from a learned model. Merely calling a model generative or obtaining a point reconstruction does not establish this output.
Integration effect: Spatial or temporal mapping
The workflow maps observations to spatial locations or temporal states, or infers transitions. Spatial coordinates used solely as input do not establish a mapping output.
Integration effect: Endpoint prediction
The profiled workflow predicts a defined phenotype, response, survival outcome or other endpoint. Generic downstream predictability of embeddings alone is insufficient.
Uncertainty output: Posterior uncertainty
The documented workflow exposes a posterior distribution, samples or summaries for biological latent quantities, parameters or missing measurements. Point/MAP estimates alone, auxiliary mixture responsibilities used only for pooling, perturbation tests and seed-to-seed variation do not qualify. A variational posterior is an approximation, not a calibration guarantee.
Uncertainty output: Endpoint predictive uncertainty
The documented workflow returns an explicit uncertainty estimate or posterior-predictive distribution for a defined endpoint. Evidential uncertainty qualifies and is distinguished from a Bayesian posterior. A point risk score, ordinary softmax probabilities or performance error bars alone do not qualify. Posterior uncertainty about missing assays is recorded in the posterior row. A noise likelihood or point reconstruction alone does not qualify.
