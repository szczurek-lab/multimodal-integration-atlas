Scope, sources and changes

The revised atlas contains 92 method profiles: 30 additions and 14 original entries moved out of the main shortlist. A separate register accounts for 31 contextual or excluded methods and resources. This is a companion to the review, not a systematic census of the field or a performance ranking.

What qualifies for the shortlist?

Methods integrating distinct measured modalities or distinct assay processes, including cross-modal prediction trained on linked modalities. Labels, augmentations, multiplex channels or a generic architecture alone do not qualify. Contextual methods remain in a separate register.

The scope includes linked imaging/text, molecular/text and imaging/clinical data, heterogeneous perturbation readouts, different molecular assays, spatial/reference assay integration, and cross-modal prediction learned from linked measurements. A method may use only one modality at prediction time. Histology-derived cell counts can contribute measured information; marker lists and outcome labels alone do not create a second modality.

How the revised axes are used

Choose data correspondence, missing inputs and observational granularity; then refine model transparency, modality decomposition, biological priors, method scope, supervision and desired outputs. Methodological building blocks can overlap. Missing-input support, generation, posterior uncertainty and calibrated prediction are separate properties.

Within one axis, checked choices are alternatives. Across axes they are combined. Selected building blocks must all be present. A profile may cover several documented workflows: its training notes determine which capabilities can be used together.

Evidence and remaining uncertainty

Publication links point to original method papers or proceedings; preprints are labelled. Dates use the online publication date when verified, otherwise the registered publication date. A year-only date is not expanded into an invented month.

86 software/documentation destinations were reachable on the review date; 6 entries have no verified public software destination. Reachability does not certify installation, maintenance or reproducibility. Repositories with similar names were not substituted without evidence that they implement the cited paper.

Practical-axis evidence is recorded separately below; unestablished details remain explicit. Independent benchmarking needs a separate evaluation source, and available uncertainty does not demonstrate calibration.

DIRAC maps each modality to point embeddings with graph encoders. Its training objective combines reconstruction, domain-adversarial, maximum mean discrepancy and multimodal-consistency losses. This construction belongs to the Deterministic optimization building block.

Internal/author-led evaluation includes simulations and retrospective evaluations and is not a clinical-validation claim.

Dataset size

Reported example scale: small <1,000; medium 1,000–99,999; large >=100,000 observational units. These thresholds are an atlas convention, not thresholds stated in the manuscript figure. Always read the unit, workflow and simulation/pretraining status. Examples do not establish minimum data needs, capacity, statistical power or equal difficulty across modalities.

Software maturity

Early: research code/archive with limited reuse infrastructure. Developing: documented setup and usage, but the full mature criteria are not established. Mature: installable package, documentation/tutorials, tests and versioned releases, or an established package-registry release with documentation and registry checks. This is an engineering-evidence classification, not age, popularity, maintenance, installation success, scientific accuracy or clinical certification. No inspectable implementation: not established.

Compute demand

Documented hardware context, not a benchmark ranking: limited = CPU/ordinary-computer example; moderate = single conventional GPU or workstation example (up to 128 GB host RAM); high = explicitly recommended HPC, more than 128 GB host RAM, >=40 GB accelerator memory, or multi-accelerator configuration. A tier describes the reported example, not a minimum. CPU support alone is insufficient. Training, inference, demo and pretraining contexts are recorded separately in notes; multiple tiers can describe different workflows.

Validation evidence

Internal = evaluation by the method authors, including their simulations, held-out data or external cohorts. Independent = a separately authored evaluation/benchmark that actually tests the method, not a review or citation. Prospective = a locked model/workflow evaluated prospectively on subsequently accrued observations/outcomes. Retrospective analyses of clinical-trial material do not qualify. Categories can coexist; absence of a verified source is not evidence of absence.

A method-level match can combine evidence from different examples or stages. It does not prove all selected properties were demonstrated simultaneously. Read the example notes before shortlisting.
